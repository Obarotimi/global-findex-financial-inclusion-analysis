# Global Financial Inclusion & Digital Finance Analysis

## Project Overview

Financial inclusion is more than simply owning a bank account.

This project analyses the World Bank Global Findex 2025 dataset to explore how access to financial services, digital finance, saving and borrowing differ across countries, regions and income groups.

The project also investigates whether high levels of financial activity reflect formal financial inclusion or greater dependence on informal financial channels.

This is an end-to-end data analysis project using Excel, SQL, Python and Power BI.

---

## Key Questions

The analysis aims to answer questions including:

1. How does financial inclusion differ across income groups and regions?
2. How does mobile money contribute to financial inclusion across different markets?
3. How do saving and borrowing behaviours differ between economies?
4. To what extent is borrowing conducted through formal versus informal channels?
5. What relationships exist between account ownership, digital payments, saving and borrowing?
6. How does Nigeria compare with Sub-Saharan Africa, its income-group peers and selected comparable economies?
7. What demographic gaps exist in financial inclusion?

These questions may evolve as the analysis progresses.

## Data Preparation & Initial Findings

Initial inspection identified several important structural characteristics of the dataset:

- The raw dataset contains hundreds of financial inclusion indicators.
- Observations include both economy-level totals and demographic subgroups.
- Missing values are not automatically interpreted as zero because indicators may not have been collected or applicable for every economy.
- Aggregate World Bank observations such as global, regional and income-group totals are included alongside individual economies and must be handled separately in country-level analysis.
- Survey coverage differs across years, meaning cross-year comparisons require attention to country coverage.

For the initial 2024 country-level analysis, aggregate observations and demographic subgroup rows are separated from individual economy observations.
